The surprise: most setups had a small edge before costs. A round trip costs about 8 Nifty points or 18 Bank Nifty points (brokerage, taxes, slippage). On intraday stops that is about a third of the risk on every trade, and it ate the whole edge.

How the test worked

  • Data: 5-minute index candles for Nifty 50 (from January 2021) and Nifty Bank (from August 2021). 15-minute and 1-hour candles are built from them. Index points stand in for futures.
  • Three periods, fixed before any rule ran: January 2021 to June 2024 to measure, July 2024 to June 2025 to check, and a final period from July 2025 that stays sealed unless a setup passes everything. None did, so it was never opened.
  • Variants: 9 setups x 2 timeframes (5 and 15 minute) x 3 targets (1.5, 2 and 3 times the risk) x 2 indices = 108.
  • Trading rules for every setup: new signals 09:30 to 14:30, every trade closed by 15:15, one trade at a time. Limit orders fill only if price trades through them. If a bar touches both stop and target, the stop counts. Setups with risk under 0.2 or over 3 times the 14-bar ATR are skipped.
  • Costs: 0.04% of the entry price per round trip (STT, exchange charges, stamp duty, GST, brokerage and slippage).
  • Luck checks: each setup was compared with 500 sets of random entries that take the same risk (random timing and random direction), and with a deflated Sharpe ratio that counts all 108 tries.

The 7 checks a setup had to pass

  1. Profit per trade above zero after costs, 2021 to mid 2024.
  2. Beats random timing (p below 0.05).
  3. Beats random direction (p below 0.05).
  4. Profit per trade above zero in the check year.
  5. Beats random timing in the check year (p below 0.10).
  6. Still profitable with costs doubled.
  7. Deflated Sharpe ratio of at least 0.95.

The 9 setups, as exact rules

A swing high is a bar whose high is above the 2 bars before and after it, confirmed 2 bars later. Structure is up when the last two swing highs and lows both rise. Shorts mirror every long rule.

1. Fair value gap retest

Bullish gap at bar i: the low of bar i is above the high of bar i-2, and bar i-1's body is at least 1 ATR. Buy limit at the low of bar i within the next 20 bars, same day. Stop below the low of bar i-1. Bearish is the mirror image.

2. Order block after a break of structure

Break up at bar i: a close above the last confirmed swing high. The order block is the last down candle in the 10 bars before i. Buy limit at its high within 30 bars, same day, unless price first closes below its low. Stop 0.1 ATR below its low.

3. Liquidity sweep reversal

Levels: the previous day's high and low, and the latest confirmed swing high and low. Bullish: a bar trades below a low level and closes back above it. Buy at that close, stop 0.1 ATR below the bar's low.

4. Change of character

Structure is down and a bar closes above the latest confirmed swing high. Buy at that close, stop 0.1 ATR below the latest confirmed swing low.

5. Inducement into the order block

As the order block rule, but at least one confirmed swing low must form above the order block after the break, and price must trade below it before the limit fills.

6. Optimal trade entry

After a break up, the impulse runs from the last confirmed swing low L to the highest high H up to the break bar. Buy limit at H minus 0.705 times (H minus L) within 30 bars, same day. Stop 0.1 ATR below L.

7. Full SMC model

1-hour structure sets the bias (longs only in an up bias, shorts only in a down bias). On the trading timeframe: a liquidity sweep against the bias, then within 12 bars a change of character with the bias, then a limit entry at the first fair value gap formed between the sweep and 3 bars after the change of character. Stop 0.1 ATR below the sweep's low.

8. Fair value gap in discount or premium

As the fair value gap rule, but longs only below the midpoint of the day's range so far (discount) and shorts only above it (premium).

9. Order block in discount or premium

As the order block rule, with the same discount and premium filter.

What this says, and what it does not

  • These mechanical versions of the setups do not make money intraday on Nifty or Bank Nifty after realistic costs, 2021 to 2025.
  • Most of them do carry a small signal before costs. The problem is that the signal is smaller than the cost of trading it.
  • This tests written rules, not a trader's judgment. Discretionary traders add context a rule cannot capture. It also does not test SMC used to place option-selling strikes.

Data notes

  • Eight Saturday mock-trading sessions found in the data (with fake prices) were removed before the final run. The numbers above are from the clean run.
  • Bank Nifty 5-minute history in the data source starts on 4 August 2021, so its first period starts there.
  • The newest price used is from June 2025.
